i. Your why

A window with warm light glowing — the way the room first looks each morning to the chaplain on duty.

In the early 1960s, in the very rooms that would one day bear his name, you lost your firstborn son after five days.

In the sixty years since, you have done something rare. You have not held that grief privately. You have built — first the Jeffrey Peter Bauer NICU, then the chaplaincy program Carol learned in that very unit to lead, then the Bauer Family Pavilion now rising on Maple Street. You have made an institution out of love that began as loss. This proposal is about how that becomes permanent.

v. What it looks like in practice

A bedside, an hour after admission. Whatever language they choose.

A young couple in Wilton learn their newborn has a complex cardiac diagnosis. Their daughter is admitted to the NICU at Norwalk. Within two hours, a chaplain trained in maternal-infant care is at their bedside — not as a religious presence imposed, but as a calm structural support, present in the language they choose.

Their daughter is stabilized. They go home in eleven days. Six months later, they find their way back to the chaplain, this time at home, in a different conversation about what came next and what didn't. They didn't know they would need that conversation when they arrived. They couldn't have known. But it was already there, waiting, because someone, decades ago, had made sure it would be.

vi. The model

— ⁂ —

Structure

Permanent endowment, separately invested, with the Foundation as trustee

Timing

Five-year pledge, with the first endowment contribution effective 2027

Recognition

Named at the Pavilion, the unit, and the fellowship — language final by family review

Governance

A Bauer Family Advisory Committee — chaired by the next generation — meets annually with the Foundation and the clinical leadership

Reporting

Annual written impact report; semi-annual conversations with the chaplaincy and NICU leadership
