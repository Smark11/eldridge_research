## 1. Snapshot

- **Full name:** Jasper Johns (no middle name commonly used in public records)
- **Age:** 95 (born May 15, 1930, in Augusta, Georgia). Turned 95 on the day before Phase 3 began.
- **Primary residence:** 170-acre estate on Low Road, Sharon, CT 06069 (owned since 1994; six contiguous parcels, converted barn studio, six houses on the property). Reported by friends and the *New York Times* to be where he prefers to wake up; characterized by former MoMA chief curator John Elderfield as "the hermit of Sharon."
- **Secondary residence(s):** Historical New York City studio (no current Manhattan residence confirmed publicly); a property in Saint Martin acquired in 1972 (status as of 2026 unconfirmed); previously Stony Point, NY (1973-1987, sold). The Sharon estate has been functionally the sole residence for ~30 years.
- **Family:** Never married. No children. Romantic and artistic partnership with Robert Rauschenberg 1954-1961 (long pre-dating Sharon residence). Brief 1965 relationship with Susan Sontag. No documented current partner. **No known heirs.** Early childhood with paternal grandparents in Allendale, SC after his parents' divorce; no surviving siblings of public record. **The succession-planning implication is dispositive:** Johns has structured his estate explicitly without family heirs, with the entire pastoral property + endowment routing to a charitable artist-residency (Low Road Sharon Inc.) on his death. There is no spousal or filial decision-maker to engage.
- **Wealth source:** Six decades of art-market sales. *Flag* (1954-55), *Target* (1955), *Three Flags*, the *Numbers* series, the *Maps*, the *Crosshatches*, the *Seasons* (1985-86), and the late *Regrets* series command transformational secondary-market prices. *Flag* sold privately to Steven A. Cohen reportedly for $110M in 2010 — a benchmark that has anchored Johns's valuation tier for fifteen years. Living-artist auction record territory.
- **Estimated net worth:** $300-500M range (confidence: **inferred**; estimates vary by source — Celebrity Net Worth $400M, others $300M, one outlier $150M). The wealth is unusually composed: a large fraction is *unsold inventory* held in the studio rather than monetized cash. The Low Road Foundation balance sheet ($217.1M assets at FY2024) is *separate from* his personal estate and is likely the principal philanthropic vehicle his estate will flow into.
- **Primary giving vehicles:** (a) **Low Road Foundation** (EIN 20-1737242, Sharon CT, $217.1M assets, $9.95M granted FY2024) — Johns is sole trustee, no co-fiduciaries listed on the foundation's 990-PF Part VIII; (b) **Low Road Sharon Inc.** (EIN 88-2015791, Sharon CT, $32.07M net assets at FY2024) — the successor 501(c)(3) that will operate the artist residency after Johns's death, with the six-director board specified in the assignment; (c) **Foundation for Contemporary Arts** (NYC, co-founded 1963 with John Cage; Johns is Chairman Emeritus since 2015, no longer principal-decision-maker). Direct personal giving and historical gifts of work to the National Gallery print collection round out the documented inventory.
- **Social/club affiliations:** None publicly visible. Johns is documented as press-averse, club-averse, and selective about appearances. He is *not* a Hotchkiss parent (no children), *not* a known regular at any Litchfield County social or country club, and has no documented relationship with the Sharon Country Club, the Sharon Historical Society, the Hotchkiss Board, or the local Lions/Rotary. Art-world institutional affiliations (MoMA, Whitney, Philadelphia Museum of Art, Royal Academy honorary, Praemium Imperiale, Presidential Medal of Freedom 2011, National Medal of Arts 1990) are all transactional or honorary, not active board service.
- **One-paragraph elevator description:** Jasper Johns is among the two or three most celebrated living American artists — a foundational figure of postwar American art whose *Flag* (1954-55) is on permanent display at MoMA and whose 2021 dual-museum retrospective at the Whitney and Philadelphia Museum of Art was the largest survey of any living artist ever mounted. At 95, he lives almost entirely on his 170-acre Sharon, CT estate, characterized by friends as a hermit who works in his converted-barn studio, eats from his garden, and rarely comes to town. He controls a $217M private foundation (Low Road Foundation) whose grantmaking is administered through him as sole trustee, and a successor entity (Low Road Sharon Inc.) whose six-director board includes his close personal aide, a Mount Sinai vice-chair philanthropist, a major contemporary-art curator, and two artist-confidants. His entire estate is structured to fund a 24-artist residency on his property post-death; there are no family heirs and no spouse. He is, on paper, Sharon Hospital's wealthiest neighbor and a near-perfect blue-sky residence-affinity prospect — but he has no documented healthcare-giving history of any tier, has never publicly identified with the Save Sharon Hospital advocacy effort, and his actual decision-making about grantmaking is opaque even to sophisticated observers.

  - *Real estate* — the 170-acre Sharon estate (six houses, studios, agricultural buildings); historically a Saint Martin property. Aggregate real estate likely $30-60M.
  - *Cash, securities, art collection (works by others)* — Johns has been a noted collector of works by Picasso, Cézanne, Duchamp, and others; valuation undocumented but plausibly $20-50M+.
  - *Low Road Foundation assets* — $217.1M, technically not personal wealth but functionally adjacent (Johns is sole trustee and the foundation receives substantial annual contributions, $16M in FY2023, which are themselves a wealth-signaling flow).
  - *Low Road Sharon Inc. assets* — $32.07M net assets at FY2024, growing rapidly through contributions ($3M in FY2022, $11.7M in FY2023, $17.6M in FY2024) — the principal source of these contributions is Johns himself and/or the Low Road Foundation, evidencing a deliberate ongoing pre-funding of the post-death residency endowment.
- **Liquidity profile:** Highly illiquid by typical metric standards. Public-equity holdings minimal/unknown. Most wealth is in artwork (his own + collected), real estate, and the two charitable vehicles. The Low Road Foundation's annual grants of ~$10M/year are funded substantially by contributions (59.5% of FY2024 revenue), implying Johns continues to fund the foundation from external resources — either personal sales income or planned-giving transfers.
- **Recent liquidity events:** **None documented as discrete transaction events.** Continuous ongoing income from work sales (Matthew Marks Gallery is his exclusive primary representative; new works displayed at Art Basel Paris 2025, including an untitled 2025 overpainted print). Continuous *grantmaking* outflows of $10M+/year via Low Road Foundation. The wealth dynamic is "high-net-worth artist with a long auction tail and a structured estate plan," not "recent liquidity event creating cultivation window."
- **Anticipated liquidity events:** **The principal anticipated liquidity event is Johns's death**, which will trigger the wholesale transfer of the estate to the Low Road Sharon Inc. residency, plus presumably substantial pour-over to the Low Road Foundation as ongoing operating endowment. *At 95, this event is structurally near-term, but its actual timing remains uncertain.* No public reporting of acute illness; Johns continues to produce new work (2025 untitled work confirmed); the most recent verifiable interview (TIME, 2021, at age 91) found him alert, witty, and discussing literary interests. The estate plan is fully documented and approved (Sharon Planning & Zoning 2017; Sharon IWWC March 2025; Sharon Planning & Zoning final approval October 2025; construction to begin early 2026).

**Strategic implication for wealth analysis.** Johns's personal wealth is large but unusually constructed: most of it is *committed by structure* to a non-hospital charitable purpose. The "available wealth" for any Nuvance solicitation is bounded by two questions: (a) what fraction of the Low Road Foundation's $10M annual grant capacity could plausibly be redirected to Sharon Hospital, and (b) whether the estate plan has flexibility for any non-residency bequests. Both questions answer at the low end of plausible.

## 3. Giving history

**Critical methodological note.** Johns's giving record is unusually opaque for a person of his wealth. The Low Road Foundation files a 990-PF annually, but the actual Schedule I (grants paid) PDF was not extractable through the WebFetch tool during research (multiple attempts; ProPublica's PDF rendering blocked content extraction). What is extractable from summary databases (Cause IQ, Instrumentl, Grantmakers.io) is the aggregate grant total and the count of grants — *not the recipient names*. The pattern across multiple summary databases is consistent and telling: Low Road Foundation reports paying out **a small number of very large grants per year** (four grants in 2024 of $10.4M, $9.9M, $7.4M, and $0.6M, totaling $28.3M, with the foundation's net charitable disbursement reported as $10.0M — suggesting some of these are inter-vehicle transfers rather than third-party arms-length grants). This is consistent with the foundation operating primarily as a *funding pipeline to Low Road Sharon Inc., the successor residency entity*, plus the Foundation for Contemporary Arts, rather than as a broad-base community grantmaker. The single named, arm's-length, externally-verifiable grant from Low Road Foundation to a third-party Sharon community organization in the past 18 months is the December 2025 $250,000 grant to the Sharon Housing Trust — and even that is *the first documented Sharon civic grant on record* across the foundation's twenty-year history.

### Pattern analysis

- **Average individual third-party arms-length grant size (documented):** $250,000 (n=1; the Sharon Housing Trust grant). The other "large" amounts shown on Low Road Foundation 990-PFs appear to be inter-vehicle transfers to Low Road Sharon Inc.
- **Largest single arm's-length grant documented:** $250,000 (Sharon Housing Trust, December 2025).
- **Time-of-year pattern:** Insufficient sample.
- **Recipient pattern (inferred from foundation activity vs. visible third-party grants):** Johns's philanthropic structure is overwhelmingly *self-directed* — the giving exists to capitalize his own legacy entities (Low Road Sharon Inc.; historically FCA where he is chairman emeritus). External grantmaking outside of art-world or self-legacy contexts is *vanishingly rare* with the Sharon Housing Trust grant being the first documented exception.
- **Multi-year pledge pattern:** The Sharon Housing Trust grant is structured as multi-year. The fact that the foundation entered into a multi-year structured grant in 2025 — and the Lakeville Journal coverage noted it was the foundation's first such Sharon civic engagement — is the strongest single signal in the dossier that Johns/his foundation directors may be in early stages of broadening Sharon community engagement.
- **Anonymous-gift signals:** Likely strong. Johns is widely characterized as preferring privacy; the foundation's invitation-only application policy and absence from public-facing donor lists suggest that *any* Johns philanthropic activity that isn't channeled through Low Road Foundation or Low Road Sharon Inc. would be effectively invisible to public databases. The gap between Johns's wealth ($300-500M+) and his visible giving (~$10M/year via foundation) leaves substantial room for anonymous direct giving — but no public evidence of it exists.

**Healthcare giving:** **$0 documented at any tier across any vehicle across all recorded years.** This is the central confidence problem in the dossier.

## 5. Motivation hypothesis

### Primary face (per Prince & File): **Altruist** (low-recognition)

Johns's documented philanthropic posture across six decades is overwhelmingly Altruist, with one structural twist that pulls the hypothesis sideways. Three pieces of evidence support the Altruist classification:

**First**, Johns has actively and consistently avoided personal recognition for his philanthropy. His co-founding of the Foundation for Contemporary Arts in 1963 with John Cage was a *deliberately collective* act — the foundation was framed as artist-mutual-aid, not as the Jasper Johns Foundation. Even in his later-life giving structure, the Sharon foundation is named "Low Road" (after the road his property sits on), not "Jasper Johns Foundation." The artist residency, "Low Road Sharon," carries the same place-name framing. This is in striking contrast to Repayer-typology donors who name centers after themselves or their families (Bauer Family Pavilion, Whittingham Cancer Center, Dyson Center for Cancer Care), and in striking contrast to Dynast-typology donors who name across generations.

**Second**, Johns's public statements consistently emphasize the *work* and the *recipients* rather than himself as benefactor. The 2021 TIME interview, in which he was asked what would constitute a perfect day, returned "few interruptions" — a privacy-and-focus answer rather than a legacy-and-recognition answer.

**Third**, the *Slice* settlement with Jéan-Marc Togodgue (2021) routed proceeds to the Foundation for Contemporary Arts rather than to Johns himself, with Johns's representative Conley Rollins explicitly framing the artist's intent as helping pay for the teen's education. The act-pattern is Altruist (recipient-focused, recognition-averse), not Socialite (event-anchored) or Communitarian (locale-pride-anchored).

### Secondary face: **emerging Communitarian** (Sharon-resident affinity)

The single new datapoint that opens a Communitarian secondary motivation is the **December 2025 Sharon Housing Trust grant** — the foundation's first documented Sharon-civic grant outside the art-world / self-legacy giving channel. At 95, with the residency-infrastructure work approved and shifting from planning to construction (early-2026 groundbreaking), Johns may be entering a brief late-life Communitarian window in which his foundation's grantmaking expands to embrace *the place where his residency will sit*. Affordable housing in Sharon is exactly the kind of place-loyalty cause that fits a Communitarian secondary motivation — the residents need housing in the town where Johns has lived for 30 years. It is plausible (though unproven) that Sharon Hospital represents a parallel place-loyalty case for the same emerging Communitarian motivation.

**Important hypothesis caveat.** The Sharon Housing Trust grant is one datapoint, six months old. It may be the start of a broader Sharon-civic shift, or it may be a one-off triggered by specific personal relationships with the Sharon Housing Trust principals. Inferring a pattern from n=1 is exactly the high-risk methodological move the Phase 0 audit warned against. **This dossier rates the Communitarian secondary motivation as plausible but unconfirmed.**

### What this means operationally

- **Cultivation approach:** Low-recognition, low-pressure, high-respect. Approaches that emphasize anonymous or quiet recognition; that frame Sharon Hospital as a *place-loyalty* cause not a *Jasper Johns Cancer Center* cause; that route through trusted intermediaries (his de facto local representative Conley Rollins; one of the Low Road Sharon Inc. board members) rather than through cold institutional approach.
- **Pitch framing:** "Sharon Hospital just had its labor & delivery unit saved by the community after the state denied closure. Northwell committed to keeping it open five years. The hospital is now the only place in the northwest corner where a baby can be born. We're trying to underwrite the next phase of women's-health investment so that promise becomes durable. We thought of the foundation because — like the housing trust gift — this is one of the places where being from Sharon matters most."
- **Anti-patterns:** Any approach involving (a) named-gift recognition at the asset level (Jasper Johns Pavilion, Johns Center for X) — anti-pattern to the consistent Altruist posture; (b) public-event-centric cultivation (galas, society dinners, donor walls) — anti-pattern to documented hermit-of-Sharon disposition; (c) high-frequency contact — Johns is reported to prefer "few interruptions"; (d) approach through institutional art-world channels (FCA board members; MoMA trustees) — these are recognition-laden and have nothing to do with Sharon civic identity. The strategy must be the opposite of how Nuvance would typically cultivate a billionaire.

## 7. Cultivation plan (12-18 months)

**Critical framing.** Given Johns's age (95), Altruist motivation, hermit disposition, and the zero-documented-healthcare-giving baseline, the cultivation plan is **not a standard 12-18 month escalation toward a transformational ask.** It is a **lower-cost discovery and qualification effort** designed to answer two questions before substantial cultivation effort is invested: (1) Does Conley Rollins (or another insider) confirm Johns has any healthcare-cause interest, conscious or latent? (2) Is there a Sharon-civic-pride angle that would land?

### Phase 1 — Qualification (months 0-4)

- **Specific recommended first-touch:** Laura initiates **two parallel low-cost conversations** in months 0-2:
  - (a) A peer-foundation introductory conversation with Foundation for Community Health leadership ("We're refreshing Sharon Hospital's case for support post-merger and would value your perspective. Are there other Sharon-area conversations we should be in?")
  - (b) A Sharon Hospital president-led civic-leader outreach to identify Sharon-CT-based connectors who know Conley Rollins. Sharon Hospital must have local volunteers, vendors, board members, or care-relationships who can name-recognize Rollins. The qualifying ask of those connectors: "If we wanted to start a quiet conversation with the Low Road Foundation about whether Sharon Hospital fits their giving — who's the right way in?"
- **Specific questions to test during qualification:**

  - Does Johns or anyone in his immediate circle have a documented patient relationship with Sharon Hospital or its affiliated physicians (the *Slice* episode implies an orthopedic surgeon — is that physician Sharon Hospital-affiliated)?
  - Is the Low Road Foundation's 2025 Sharon Housing Trust grant the start of a Sharon-civic broadening, or is it idiosyncratic?
  - Is there any history of family-member care at Sharon Hospital — Johns has no immediate family but may have extended family or close-friend-care experiences?
- **Decision point at month 4:** **Either advance to Phase 2 (cautious cultivation) or drop and reallocate effort to higher-priority candidates.** The decision rule:
  - *Advance* if (a) the Conley Rollins connector path is identified and active; OR (b) any insider confirms a healthcare-cause receptivity signal; OR (c) the Foundation for Community Health (or another peer Sharon institution) indicates strong support for a Sharon Hospital cultivation move.
  - *Drop* if (a) no Conley Rollins access path materializes after 4 months of effort; AND (b) no healthcare-receptivity signal emerges. The opportunity-cost on Phase 3 effort is high; this candidate must qualify forward or be replaced.

### Phase 2 — Cultivation (months 4-12) — *conditional on Phase 1 advance*

- **Recommended touchpoints, with specific people from Nuvance to involve at each:**
  - Month 4-5: Initial quiet meeting between Laura, Sharon Hospital president, and Conley Rollins (or whoever the Phase 1 qualification surfaced as the right connector). *Setting: an early-morning coffee at a Sharon location, not a hospital function. No clinical leaders, no system CEO. Light footprint.*
  - Month 6-8: If the relationship advances, a quiet site visit. The frame should be Sharon Hospital as Sharon-place-loyalty institution, not as part of a 28-hospital Northwell system. Possible co-attendee: Foundation for Community Health leadership (peer foundation present as a credibility-by-association move, not as solicitor).
  - Month 9-12: A second touchpoint at the foundation level — could be a *Low Road Foundation* invitation-only round in which Johns's representative receives a Sharon Hospital case-for-support that is structured to be place-loyalty-anchored, not naming-anchored. The case-for-support framing must explicitly include the L&D protection story and the women's-health-investment opportunity post-merger.
- **Recommended events or convenings:** **None of the gala/dinner type.** Johns will not attend. Conley Rollins may attend a Sharon civic event if framed as such (e.g., a Sharon Hospital community-pride briefing co-hosted with Foundation for Community Health), but the cultivation strategy must avoid all society-page framings.
- **Pacing:** Slow. 3-4 touches across 8 months. Quality over quantity.

### Phase 3 — Solicitation (months 12-18) — *conditional on Phase 2 advance*

- **Suggested ask amount:** **$1-5M, structured as multi-year, with no naming requirement.** The Sharon Housing Trust grant ($250K multi-year) is the foundation's most recent Sharon-civic precedent and sets the implicit anchor at that order of magnitude. A $1M ask is a 4× scaling of that precedent — credible if cultivation surfaces healthcare-receptivity. A $5M ask is the upper end of plausible if the case-for-support uniquely articulates a Sharon-women's-health-protection opportunity. The original Phase 2 framing of $1-10M is bracketed at the realistic low end given the new information that the foundation has *never* given to healthcare and has only just begun externally-visible Sharon civic giving.
- **Suggested named opportunity:** **No personal naming.** This is the single most important departure from typical Nuvance major-gift framing for this candidate. The Altruist motivation precludes a Jasper Johns Pavilion or Johns Center for Women's Health. Possible structures:
  - "Sharon Hospital Women's Health Fund" with the foundation as anchor donor (un-named at the gift level)
  - "Sharon Community Maternity Initiative" framed as a place-loyalty civic asset (Foundation for Community Health as co-funder; Low Road Foundation as second co-funder)
  - A *Low Road-named* component if the foundation does want recognition — e.g., a Low Road Maternity Garden / Low Road Family Resource Center — extending the "Low Road" place-brand the way the foundation and residency already do. This would be acceptable to Altruist motivation while satisfying any minimal recognition obligation.
- **Who should be at the ask:** Laura + Sharon Hospital president + (potentially) Foundation for Community Health leadership as peer/credibility figure. **NOT** John D'Angelo, Michelle Robertson, or any Northwell-system leader. The system-level overlay is anti-pattern to Johns's local-civic affinity case. **NOT** Nuvance medical-clinical leadership unless the cultivation has uniquely surfaced a women's-health clinician who Conley Rollins respects.
- **Setting recommendation:** Sharon-based. Possibly at Foundation for Community Health offices (peer-foundation credibility) or at Sharon Hospital with no clinical-tour overlay. **NOT** at Vassar Brothers, Norwalk, or Danbury (these are not the affinity institutions).
- **Specific opening, bridge, and ask language:**
  - **Opening:** "Mr. Rollins, thank you for the time. We're here because Sharon Hospital was nearly closed eighteen months ago. The state denied the closure. Northwell committed to keeping the labor-and-delivery unit open for five years under the merger agreement. That commitment ends in 2031. We have a 60-month window to make Sharon Hospital structurally durable — and we believe a small set of Sharon-loyal philanthropic anchors is what makes that durable."
  - **Bridge:** "The Sharon Housing Trust gift last December was a signal to a lot of us in the local nonprofit ecosystem that Low Road Foundation may be expanding its footprint of Sharon civic engagement. Sharon Hospital is the other indispensable Sharon civic institution. If a similar quiet investment from the foundation could anchor the women's-health protection commitment — without a building name, without recognition, in the structural form the foundation prefers — that's what we'd like to ask the trustees to consider."
  - **Ask:** "We're asking the foundation to consider a $1-5M multi-year structured grant — a four-to-ten-times scaling of the Sharon Housing Trust commitment — anchored to the women's-health line at Sharon Hospital, with the gift structured as the foundation prefers regarding recognition. We'd be grateful for any conversation that surfaces whether this aligns with the foundation's priorities."

### Phase 4 — Stewardship (months 18+)

- **Recommended named-recognition event style:** *None.* Reporting should be quiet — a quarterly letter to the foundation, no press releases without explicit consent, no donor-wall placement.
- **Impact-reporting cadence:** Annual written impact report to the foundation, simple, factual, without ceremonial framing.
- **Clinical-leadership ongoing-access plan:** A relationship between Sharon Hospital president and Conley Rollins (or the relevant Low Road Foundation contact) — not Johns directly. The institutional-level relationship survives Johns's eventual death by transferring to the Low Road Sharon Inc. board's continued grantmaking authority (the foundation will continue past Johns; his death does not zero out the cultivation).

## 8. Risk flags

- **Active litigation involving the candidate:** None of consequence identified. The 2013-2014 case of Johns's former studio assistant James Meyer (sentenced to 18 months in federal prison for theft of Johns works) is fully adjudicated and 10+ years past resolution. The 2021 Togodgue / *Slice* dispute resolved via August 2021 settlement (proceeds to FCA). Both are reputationally clean for Johns; in fact, the Togodgue resolution arguably enhances his Altruist credibility.
- **Family disputes affecting philanthropic decision-making:** *None possible* — no family. This is unusual but eliminates an entire risk category.
- **Public controversies that could create reputational risk:** The 2021 Togodgue episode was briefly negative-press but resolved cleanly. No other ongoing controversies. The IWWC and Planning & Zoning approval process for Low Road Sharon Inc. surfaced some local opposition (concerns about Lovers Lane traffic, stream gradient) but resolved with full approval by October 2025 — minor friction, not reputational damage.
- **Competing institutional loyalties — board memberships, recent gifts to competitors:**
  - **Material:** Joel Ehrenkranz (Low Road Sharon Inc. director) is Vice Chairman of Mount Sinai Medical Center board. Mount Sinai is not a *direct* Sharon Hospital competitor (Mount Sinai is Manhattan tertiary care) but is a strong NYC-medical-school loyalty inside the residency entity's governance.
  - **Material:** Carlos Basualdo, Cecily Brown, Julian Lethbridge — all art-world directors with no documented healthcare loyalties but also no place-loyalty to Sharon CT (Basualdo is now Dallas; Brown is London/NYC; Lethbridge is NYC/CT but Anne Bass estate in South Kent, not Sharon).
  - **Modest:** Johns himself was hospitalized historically (the *Slice* episode implies an orthopedic surgeon relationship); the surgeon's institutional affiliation is undocumented and could be Hospital for Special Surgery (Manhattan) or Berkshire Medical Center or any number of alternatives. If Johns has a personal physician relationship at a competing institution, that surfaces in cultivation.
- **Recent declined asks elsewhere:** None documented. The foundation's invitation-only policy makes "declined ask" a meaningless category — they don't accept unsolicited requests, so no public decline can occur.
- **Health or life-situation flags:**
  - **Material — age 95.** Cognitive and physical capacity at 95 are highly variable. The 2021 TIME interview (at age 91) was conducted by email, not in person — Johns is documented as preferring written and remote communication. *Whether Johns himself is making grantmaking decisions in 2026, or whether decisions are being made by Conley Rollins and the Low Road Sharon Inc. board with Johns as a notional approver, is genuinely unclear from public sources.* This is the single biggest unknown in the dossier and is dispositive for cultivation strategy.
  - **The "decision-rights question" called out in the assignment is real and unresolved.** If Johns himself directs grants, cultivation must approach Johns through trusted intermediaries (Conley Rollins) and accommodate his preference for written communication. If Conley Rollins (or the broader Low Road Sharon Inc. board) is the de facto decision-maker, cultivation can be more conventionally interpersonal — but must navigate the Ehrenkranz / Mount Sinai loyalty within that board.
- **Estate-plan irreversibility:** The estate plan is *publicly announced and structurally near-complete* — full Sharon municipal approval secured, construction beginning early 2026, the successor entity (Low Road Sharon Inc.) fully operational with growing reserves. The wholesale-estate-to-residency commitment is unlikely to be reversed. Any Nuvance ask must operate within the residual flexibility — the foundation's $10M/year grantmaking capacity and any non-residency portion of the personal estate — *not* attempt to redirect the residency-bound assets.

## 9. Novel-strategy applicability

- **G.1 DAF activation:** **Not applicable.** Johns uses a private foundation structure, not a DAF.
- **G.2 Family-office direct engagement:** **Applicable in a limited form.** Conley Rollins functions as a family-office-equivalent for Johns. The "family office" doctrine in the playbook implies professionalized intermediary engagement — and Rollins is the available analog.
- **G.3 Peer-cohort:** **Limited applicability.** A Sharon-Hospital cohort of Litchfield-resident philanthropists (Annette de la Renta in Kent, Peter May in Bridgewater, the Hotchkiss board figures Tim Sullivan/Robert Gould, Henry & Marie-Josée Kravis in Sharon) could in theory pull Johns into a peer-conversation — but the Altruist disposition resists peer-conformity dynamics, and Johns has no documented social participation in any Litchfield peer cohort. *Foundation for Community Health is the cleaner peer-cohort node*: a Sharon-philanthropic-peers convening hosted by FCH leadership could be a natural setting.
- **G.4 Patient-advocate council:** **Inapplicable** — Johns is not a patient-advocate, has no documented patient history at Sharon Hospital, and would not occupy such a council seat.
- **G.5 Advisor-ecosystem cultivation:** **Highly applicable.** This is the central recommendation. Conley Rollins is the advisor; cultivating him is cultivating the principal. The pathway parallels how sophisticated family-office advisors are engaged at peer institutions. Laura should treat Rollins as a long-term-relationship advisor counterparty.
- **G.6 Local-community-leader-anchor strategy:** **Highly applicable.** The Foundation for Community Health is the natural local-leader anchor. FCH leadership convening a quiet conversation with Low Road Foundation about the Sharon civic-health ecosystem post-merger is the precise move.
- **G.7 Estate-planning-attorney engagement:** **Limited applicability.** Johns's estate plan is publicly documented and locked in via the Low Road Sharon Inc. structure. The residual flexibility for non-residency bequests is unclear; if substantial, the cultivation pathway could include a charitable-bequest dimension. But the dominant assets are committed.
- **G.8 Anonymous-gift accommodation:** **Highly applicable.** Johns's Altruist disposition aligns with anonymous or low-recognition giving. Nuvance must explicitly offer this structure. The "Low Road Maternity Fund" framing (place-name not personal-name) is the maximum-recognition tier the foundation is likely to accept.
- **G.9 Bequest-conversion cultivation:** **Limited applicability.** The estate is largely committed by structure.
- **G.10 Cause-storytelling adaptation:** **Highly applicable.** The Sharon L&D advocacy story is the single most compelling civic-pride narrative available for this candidate. The Save Sharon Hospital community fight (2018-2024), the OHS denial (Feb 2024), the Northwell 5-year preservation commitment, the local political organizing — all of this is a community-pride story Johns can plausibly relate to as a 30-year Sharon resident. Cause-storytelling for this candidate is *Sharon as community*, not *Nuvance as system*.
