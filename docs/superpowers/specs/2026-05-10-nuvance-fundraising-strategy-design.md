## 1. Overview

Produce a comprehensive, research-driven major-gift strategy package for Laura Eldridge's use at Nuvance Health Foundation. Laura is a credentialed CFRE working a portfolio of approximately 250 billionaire prospects, with a stated institutional fundraising aspiration in the $500M/year range.

The deliverable is independent research and strategy — not based on Laura's internal prospect list. The package gives her a second opinion (validating or extending her own list), deep dossiers on independently identified top candidates, and a tailored playbook for cultivating and soliciting them.

### Context flags

- **$500M/year is an exceptional target for a regional health system.** For comparison, only top-3 medical philanthropic institutions in the U.S. (Memorial Sloan Kettering, Mayo, Cleveland Clinic, MD Anderson) raise at that level, with hundreds of staff and decades of cultivation infrastructure. Most regional health systems raise $5-50M/year. The strategy is calibrated as a transformational mega-gift play that could *contribute meaningfully* to that aspiration, with honest assessment of institutional gaps where they exist.
- **Laura is a credentialed CFRE.** The package is *not* a fundraising primer; it's specific research and tailored strategy for her specific cohort.
- **Independent research.** We do not request Laura's list. The deliverable is value-additive precisely because it's an independent perspective.

## 2. Strategic approach

**Affinity-first prospect identification, with calibrated wealth tiers.**

Standard "billionaire list" frame is the wrong starting point. The empirical evidence on $10M+ hospital gifts is unambiguous: the dominant driver is the **grateful patient / Repayer motive** (per Prince & File's *Seven Faces of Philanthropy* typology), with legacy/mortality salience as a strong secondary. Affinity to the specific institution beats raw wealth as a predictor of major gifts.

We therefore reverse the lens:
1. Identify candidates with documented affinity signals to Nuvance Health or its predecessor institutions
2. Filter that affinity-rich population for sufficient wealth
3. Calibrate the final 10 across two wealth tiers, summing to exactly 10:
   - **4-5 billionaires** with strong service-area ties
   - **5-6 high-end centi-millionaires** ($300M-$999M) where Nuvance can plausibly be their philanthropic flagship
   - Acceptable splits: 4 billionaires + 6 centi-millionaires, or 5 billionaires + 5 centi-millionaires. Final split is determined by where the affinity-scored evidence is strongest, not pre-allocated.

Centi-millionaires often make *larger* gifts to regional institutions than billionaires do, because they receive top-tier recognition (named building, board chair) unavailable to them at flagship hospitals (NYP, MSK, etc.).

## 3. Research methodology

### Geographic anchor
Nuvance Health serves the Hudson Valley NY and Western CT — one of the wealthiest commuter belts in America (Greenwich/Fairfield hedge-fund corridor, Westchester, Dutchess County estate country, the CT/NY/MA second-home belt). Facilities include Danbury Hospital, Norwalk Hospital, Vassar Brothers (Poughkeepsie), Northern Dutchess, Putnam, Sharon, New Milford.

### Affinity signal stack (six signals, scored 1-5 per candidate)
1. **Documented Nuvance connection** — board service (current/past), major patient experience, executive relationship, predecessor-institution loyalty (Vassar Brothers / Danbury / Norwalk / Health Quest legacies)
2. **Residential ties** — primary or secondary residence in service area
3. **Healthcare giving history** — prior hospital gifts, especially regional; named gifts >$5M anywhere
4. **Cause affinity** — gifts to clinical specialties Nuvance is strong in or expanding (cancer, cardiac, women's health, neurosciences)
5. **Giving vehicle sophistication** — active private foundation, DAF activity, multi-year pledge history
6. **Life-stage signals** — age 60+, recent liquidity event, succession planning visible, family situation suggesting estate-planning readiness

### Public data sources
- Forbes 400 + Bloomberg Billionaires Index (geographic filter)
- ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer + Cause IQ (990-PF foundation grant history)
- SEC filings (insider holdings, recent liquidity events)
- News archives — major-gift announcements, board appointments, obituaries (estate-planning signals)
- Local/regional business journals (Westchester Magazine, CT Insider, HV Biz Journal)
- Foundation annual reports across the region (NYP, Greenwich Hospital, Northwell, Yale-New Haven, MSK) — to map giving-pattern context
- LinkedIn (board service patterns)
- Nuvance Health Foundation's own 990s and public materials

### Exclusions
- No hard-to-verify names or rumor-sourced claims
- No candidates whose wealth is structurally illiquid with no near-term liquidity path (unless other path exists)
- No candidates with documented hostility to Nuvance or predecessor institutions
- No relying on second-hand reports without primary-source verification

### Output structure
- **Phase 1 output:** Long list of 40-60 names with preliminary affinity scoring
- **Phase 2 output:** Short-list of 10 with full justification
- Each candidate includes a **motivation hypothesis** (which of the Seven Faces dominates) informed by giving history, public statements, and life events

### Donor-psychology research base
The methodology draws on:
- Prince & File, *The Seven Faces of Philanthropy* (1994 — still the canonical typology): Communitarian, Devout, Investor, Socialite, Repayer, Altruist, Dynast
- Bekkers & Wiepking's eight-mechanism meta-analysis of giving motivation
- The hospital-mega-gift empirical evidence base — Repayer motive dominates, legacy/mortality salience secondary
- Sargeant & Shang's research on solicitation effectiveness (peer-asked vs. development-officer-asked)
- Anchoring research on gift-size determination (donors give within 10-30% of specific ask amount; vague asks underperform by ~40%)

## 4. Deliverables

Eight artifacts, ordered by leverage:

### Artifact 1 — Individual dossiers (10 × 6-8 pages)
The core research product. For each candidate:
- Snapshot (age, residence, family, wealth source, estimated net worth, giving vehicles)
- Wealth & liquidity analysis (where money is, what's liquid, recent/upcoming liquidity events)
- Giving history (every documented gift $100K+, organized by cause and recipient, patterns called out)
- Affinity signals scored 1-5 on the six-signal stack
- Motivation hypothesis (which of the Seven Faces dominates, with evidence)
- Warm-intro paths (named connectors ranked by likely effectiveness)
- Cultivation plan (12-18 month sequence with specific ask amount and named-opportunity recommendation)
- Risk flags (litigation, family disputes, controversies, competing institutional loyalties, recent declined asks)
- Per-candidate novel-strategy applicability (which of Playbook Section G strategies fit)
- Full source list per claim

### Artifact 2 — Warm-intro network map
Visual graph rendering every plausible path from Laura's known network (her board, CEO, foundation board, predecessor-institution legacy donors) to each of the 10 candidates. Built as an interactive HTML visualization. **This is the access unlock** — billionaire-tier prospect access is the dominant operational bottleneck; a documented intro map is rare and high-value.

### Artifact 3 — Benchmarking memo on comparable mega-gifts (15-25 pages)
Deep-dive on 8-12 of the most relevant mega-gifts to regional health systems in the past 15 years. For each: gift size, donor profile and motivation, cultivation length, institutional moves that landed it, post-gift stewardship pattern. Pattern extraction at the end — what worked and why. Studied cases will likely include Greenwich Hospital, Hospital for Special Surgery, Northwell, Stamford Health, Mount Sinai (Icahn), Penn Medicine, NYU Langone, Yale-New Haven.

### Artifact 4 — Per-candidate "open with this" cheat sheets (1 page each × 10)
Printable one-page operational prep card per candidate: what you know about them, three opening questions tailored to their giving history, two reference points to mention (people, gifts, places they care about), the single motivation hypothesis to test in the first meeting. Sniper-level prep Laura can carry into meetings.

### Artifact 5 — Ranked short-list with cross-reference matrix
The 10 candidates ranked in priority order. For each: name, estimated net worth, primary affinity hypothesis, suggested gift range, recommended first-meeting purpose, recommended cultivation lead (Laura, CEO, board chair, etc.), warm-intro path summary. Designed so Laura can lay it next to her 250 and immediately see overlap (validation), net-new prospects, and ranking differences.

### Artifact 6 — Tailored strategy playbook (25-40 pages)
Synthesis document. *Not* a generic fundraising primer. Six sections — see Section 5 below.

### Artifact 7 — Executive summary (2-3 pages)
Skimmable framing. Written last, after everything is settled. Designed to be re-sharable up the chain to Laura's director, CEO, or board chair without editing.

### Artifact 8 — Designed presentation deck
Visually distinctive, on-brand presentation deck. Built using the `frontend-design` skill — HTML-based, designed with real design rigor (not a generic PowerPoint template). Customizable per-donor (slots for swapping in donor-specific motivation framing and named opportunity). Designed to be both presented and left behind, since mega-gift conversations rarely close on first meeting.

## 5. Strategy playbook contents

Seven sections (~25-40 pages):

### Section A — Donor psychology, applied
- Brief framework (Prince's Seven Faces, Bekkers & Wiepking mechanisms, hospital-mega-gift evidence base)
- Per-candidate motivation profile mapping
- Operational implications by face (Repayer: build cultivation around their care experience; Dynast: family naming and multigenerational involvement; Investor: impact metrics and tax-strategic structuring; Communitarian: world-class-hospital-in-our-community framing)
- Anti-patterns by face

### Section B — Cultivation cadence
A research-backed 12-24 month cadence calibrated to a $50M+ ask:
- **Phase 1: Qualification (months 0-3)** — establish interest, confirm capacity, map motivations
- **Phase 2: Cultivation (months 3-12)** — clinical leadership introductions, site visits, peer-donor events, value-additive engagement
- **Phase 3: Solicitation (months 12-18)** — formal ask (CEO + board chair + peer donor, donor's setting, specific number/opportunity/timeline, written proposal)
- **Phase 4: Stewardship (months 18+)** — recognition events, impact reporting cadence, ongoing access to clinical leadership; positions for a second (usually larger) gift

### Section C — Appointment-getting tactics
- Warm-intro leverage (referencing Artifact 2)
- "No-ask" first meeting framing (advice/insight requests outperform solicitation framing)
- Calendar leverage points (year-end tax planning, post-liquidity events, post-personal-health-events, board-cycle transitions)
- Gatekeeper management (executive assistants, family-office gatekeepers, foundation program officers)
- Re-engagement after "no, not now, or radio silence" (timing, what to change)

### Section D — Pitch architecture
Structural framework for the $50M+ solicitation conversation:
- Opening: the donor's why, not yours (first 15 minutes always center the candidate's experience)
- Bridge: their why → specific opportunity, tied to motivation hypothesis
- Specific ask: exact amount, exact named opportunity, exact timeline, exact pledge structure
- Anticipated objection handling ("why Nuvance over NYP?", "we already give to MSK", "we need to talk to advisors")
- The close: silence after the ask (development officer who speaks first loses ~30% of expected gift size)
- Walk-away clarity (what's agreed, when next, what the candidate commits to in interim)

### Section E — Gift table
Sample mega-campaign table to reach $500M:
- 1 gift @ $100M+ (lead)
- 2 gifts @ $50M
- 4 gifts @ $25M
- 8 gifts @ $10M
- ...
Includes honest institutional assessment: does Nuvance's current case for support sustain a $500M campaign? What would need to be true institutionally? This is the most likely place we surface uncomfortable findings; we surface them clearly rather than hiding them.

### Section F — Risk register and contingency plans
- Top-ranked candidate declines or goes cold — who's next, why
- Competing institution (NYP, Yale-New Haven, MSK) in active cultivation — complement vs. compete
- Candidate life situation changes (death, divorce, illness, financial reversal) during cultivation
- Institutional case-for-support shifts mid-cultivation

### Section G — Novel and frontier strategies
Going beyond standard CFRE playbook. Flagged as evidence-backed or speculative.

**Evidence-backed at top institutions:**
1. DAF activation — many candidates have $10M-$500M in undirected DAFs; structurally easier ask than new commitment
2. Family-office direct engagement — shorten cycle time via family-office philanthropy director
3. Peer-cohort/circle asks — 3-5 peer donors giving together as a founding cohort
4. Patient-advocate council at scale — grateful-patient motive as peer cultivation engine (NYU Langone, Cedars-Sinai, MSK models)
5. Donor's-advisor-ecosystem cultivation — building relationships with candidate's wealth managers, trust officers, estate attorneys

**More speculative:**
6. Donor-as-co-architect — co-design of named opportunity (Stanford, MIT for $100M+ gifts)
7. Impact-investment / hybrid grant-loan structures (Investor-type donors)
8. Naming 2.0 — named clinical-trial cohorts, multigenerational programs, naming with sunset provisions
9. Convening-as-cultivation — recurring topical convening hosted around the donor (Mayo, Cleveland Clinic, MD Anderson)
10. AI-instrumented donor intelligence — continuous monitoring for liquidity events, life events, board transitions

Each dossier (Artifact 1) flags which of these fit the specific candidate.

## 6. Phasing and sequencing

Eight phases. Some run in parallel. Explicit checkpoints with Mark before heavy-effort phases.

### Phase 0 — Institutional context & case-for-support audit
Deep read of Nuvance Health public materials. 990 filings, strategic plan, annual reports, board composition, recent major gifts, capital projects. Predecessor-institution legacy mapping. Identification of plausible naming opportunities by tier ($10M / $25M / $50M / $100M / $250M). Honest assessment of whether Nuvance's current case for support sustains a $500M campaign and what institutional gaps exist.
- **Output:** Internal context memo
- **Checkpoint:** Share key findings with Mark before proceeding

### Phase 1 — Long-list generation
Apply geographic and wealth screens; cross-reference healthcare giving; build scored long list of 40-60 names; triangulate wealth estimates across at least two independent sources; pre-filter for disqualifying flags.
- **Output:** Scored long list with brief per-name justification
- **Checkpoint:** Mark reviews; can add or exclude names

### Phase 2 — Short-list selection
Deeper-dive screening on top 15-20 from long list; cross-reference every documented gift $1M+ to hospital/medical institutions in past 15 years; triage to final 10 (4-5 billionaires + 5-6 centi-millionaires).
- **Output:** Ranked short-list with paragraph rationale per candidate
- **Checkpoint:** Mark reviews and approves the 10 before heavy dossier phase

### Phase 3 — Deep dossiers (heaviest phase)
Full 6-8 page dossier per candidate.
- **Output:** 10 fully sourced, triangulated dossiers
- **Checkpoint:** First 2 dossiers shared as samples before producing remaining 8 (course-correct format/depth/emphasis early)

### Phase 4 — Benchmarking memo (parallel with Phase 3)
- **Output:** 15-25 page memo, structured as pattern library
- No checkpoint; delivered with final package

### Phase 5 — Tactical artifacts
Warm-intro network map (visual HTML graph), per-candidate cheat sheets (10 × 1 page), cross-reference matrix.
- **Output:** Three tactical artifacts

### Phase 6 — Strategy playbook synthesis
Pull Phases 0-5 into the playbook structure in Section 5 above. Per-candidate strategy integration.
- **Output:** 25-40 page tailored playbook
- **Checkpoint:** Full playbook draft for Mark's review before final polish

### Phase 7 — Executive summary + presentation deck
Executive summary written last after everything is settled. Designed presentation deck built using `frontend-design` skill — HTML-based, visually distinctive, on-brand with Nuvance's visual identity, customizable per-donor.
- **Output:** Executive summary + designed deck

### Effort estimate
Full scope: roughly 6-10 weeks of focused work for one person at high quality.

## 7. Data limitations (surfaced upfront, not hidden)

Every dossier will flag what's confidently known vs. inferred vs. unknown. Known limits:

- **DAF grants are not public.** We can identify *that* a candidate has a DAF, but not their grants out of it.
- **Wealth estimates for non-Forbes-listed individuals are inferred**, not measured — typically accurate within a factor of 2x.
- **Anonymous gifts are common** at the mega-gift level — some giving is invisible to us.
- **990-PF data lags 18-24 months** — we may miss recent foundation grant activity.
- **Family-office structures are often opaque** — not always discoverable from public sources.

## 8. Out of scope

Deliberately excluded to keep the package focused and high-leverage:

- Annual giving strategy
- Planned-giving program design (touched on as candidate-applicable strategy, not as program build)
- Board development / governance
- Campaign infrastructure (CRM, gift processing, stewardship operations)
- Direct outreach to candidates (this is Laura's role, not Mark's)
- Sharing of Laura's internal portfolio data
- Any communication with Laura prior to Mark's review of the final package

## 9. Success criteria

Success is evaluated in two layers because we cannot see Laura's internal list during the work.

### Internally verifiable during the work (we can confirm before delivery)
1. Each of the 10 dossiers triangulates wealth, giving history, and affinity signals against at least two independent primary sources, and explicitly labels confidence (known / inferred / unknown)
2. Each dossier surfaces at least one warm-intro path with a named individual connector and rationale
3. The benchmarking memo covers 8-12 comparable regional-health-system mega-gifts with pattern extraction at the end
4. The strategy playbook contains per-candidate strategy integration (not generic prose), with each of the 10 candidates referenced explicitly in Sections A through G
5. The case-for-support audit (Phase 0) surfaces at least one honest institutional finding that Nuvance leadership should address — whether or not they choose to
6. The presentation deck is institutional-quality (visually distinctive, on-brand, customizable per-donor)

### Verifiable only post-hoc, via Laura's feedback after delivery
7. Laura finds at least 3 of the 10 candidates surfaced are either net-new to her list or significantly reranked vs. her own ranking
8. At least 5 of the warm-intro paths in the dossiers are net-new to her
9. Laura applies at least 2 cultivation patterns from the benchmarking memo
10. The presentation deck is used in at least one real solicitation meeting

Criteria 7-10 are aspirational outcome measures. They cannot be tested during the work; they're recorded here so the eventual feedback loop with Laura has explicit targets.

The package is *not* successful if it reads like a generic fundraising consulting deliverable that could have been produced for any health system. Specificity to Nuvance and to these 10 candidates is the bar.
