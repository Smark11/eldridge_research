# Case Study — Victoria and Lloyd Goldman gift to Northwell Health / Lenox Hill Hospital (Nov 2023)

**Gift size:** Lifetime giving to Northwell reaches **$100 million**; the Nov 2023 lead gift naming the Goldman Pavilion is described by Northwell as the **"largest single gift in [Northwell's] history."** Exact 2023 commitment not publicly disclosed; reporting characterizes the naming gift as **"eight-figure"** within a $450M project. (Northwell press release Nov 15, 2023; Northwell Foundation Pacesetters page; Lifestyles Magazine.) [1][2][3][6]
**Type:** Lead / Principal — Pavilion-naming, multi-decade cumulative
**Compiled:** 2026-05-11
**Status of relevance:** **THE benchmark case for the seven-candidate dossier set.** Lloyd Goldman is the **first cousin** of Amy Goldman Fowler (Phase 3 candidate #6). He has been a Northwell trustee for years, sits on the Feinstein Institutes board, and serves on the campaign executive committee of Northwell's just-completed $1.4B "Outpacing the Impossible" campaign. This document is the operational template for the warm-intro pathway from Lloyd → Amy.

## 1. The gift in one paragraph

On November 14, 2023, at Northwell Health's annual Constellation Gala at the Metropolitan Museum of Art, Northwell announced that longtime trustees Victoria and Lloyd Goldman had made the **largest single gift in the health system's history**, naming the **Victoria and Lloyd Goldman Medical Pavilion** (also reported as the Victoria and Lloyd Goldman Health Care Pavilion) — a 15-story, 200,000-square-foot, $450 million outpatient complex under construction at 1345 Third Avenue between East 76th and 77th Streets, immediately adjacent to Lenox Hill Hospital, slated to open in 2026.[1][2][6][12][16] The gift also funds the **Victoria and Lloyd Goldman Professorship** as an endowed-faculty component. With this commitment, the Goldmans' **lifetime giving to Northwell crossed the $100 million threshold**.[1][2] Lloyd Goldman is president of BLDG Management — a Manhattan real estate platform managing $2 billion+ in property descended from the original Sol Goldman / Irving Goldman portfolio — and is the **first cousin of Amy Goldman Fowler**, Sol Goldman's middle daughter, who is Phase 3 dossier candidate #6.[7][9][18]

### Primary motivation (Prince's framework)

**Primary face: Dynast.** Secondary faces: **Repayer / Communitarian** (with a strong **Devout** through-line tied to Jewish-community giving).

**Evidence:**
- Almost every major named institutional act memorializes a family member: the **Joyce and Irving Goldman Medical School** (parents); **Victoria and Lloyd Goldman Pavilion** (married couple); **Goldman-Sonnenfeldt School** (sister and brother-in-law); **Joyce and Irving Goldman Family Foundation** (parents). The foundation's own history page foregrounds the Talmudic principle "Whoever teaches his children teaches not only his children, but also his children's children" — explicit dynastic-transmission language.[23]
- Direct dynastic quote from Victoria Goldman at the 2023 gala: **"Our journey with Northwell began many years ago with Lloyd's childhood hospital, North Shore University Hospital, and continues with Lenox Hill Hospital."**[1][4] This is textbook Repayer-overlay onto a Dynast frame: the *family's continuity with the institution* is the load-bearing narrative element.
- Lloyd's own framing reinforces both faces: **"It is an honor for us to give back to our community through this new pavilion. We believe contributing to health care will help shape a brighter future for the people who come to Northwell for their care."**[1][2] *Community* language is Communitarian; *give back* is Repayer.
- The eJewishPhilanthropy interviews (2024-2025) document a deliberate post-October-7 shift toward *named, public* giving — Lloyd describes his father as having given anonymously, but Lloyd has decided communities should "recognize Jewish contributions to society."[20][21] This is dynastic-legacy maturation, not socialite/show-money giving.

## 3. Recipient institution profile (at time of gift)

### Northwell Health (November 2023)

- **Scale:** Largest healthcare provider in New York State. As of late 2023 reporting: 21+ hospitals (expanding to 28+ post-Nuvance merger May 2026), ~85,000 employees, ~$16B+ revenue, ~6,500-physician medical group, system-wide network.[16]
- **Leadership:** Michael J. Dowling, President and CEO (since 2002; stepped down October 1, 2025, succeeded by John D'Angelo, MD).[2][30] Mark Solazzo, EVP/COO (since 2005). Brian Lally, President of Northwell Foundation and Chief Development Officer.[27][29]
- **Campaign context:** "Outpacing the Impossible" — Northwell's first comprehensive campaign, **$1.4B goal exceeded** (closed 2024 with 209,000+ donors). The Goldman gift was the largest single anchor of this campaign and was structured to support the headline Manhattan-expansion ask.[6][26][27]
- **Manhattan footprint:** Lenox Hill Hospital (Upper East Side), Manhattan Eye Ear & Throat Hospital, Lenox Health Greenwich Village (since 2025 reorganized as Northwell Greenwich Village Hospital), and 100+ outpatient sites.[6][16]

### Lenox Hill Hospital specifically

- 450-bed teaching hospital at 100 East 77th Street; member of Northwell since 2010.
- **Daniel Baker, MD, MBA** — Executive Director since 2022 (joined Lenox Hill 2007 as emergency physician, rose through Northwell physician leadership program).[28]
- Mid-2020s Lenox Hill is the focus of a **$1.6-2.5B comprehensive redevelopment** approved by NYC Council August 2023 (modified tower to 370 ft; 9-year construction timeline). The $450M Goldman Pavilion at 1345 Third Avenue is one component of that broader Lenox Hill revitalization.

### Case for support

The pavilion was pitched as a **Manhattan-anchored, women-and-people-of-color cancer + cardiac + neuroscience destination outpatient center**, programmatically distinct from Lenox Hill's inpatient redevelopment — explicitly designed to bring Northwell Cancer Institute services to Manhattan in a single building. Programmatic anchors stated in launch communications: medical oncology, radiation oncology, surgical oncology; **dedicated women's center; programs for endometrial cancer, triple-negative breast cancer, and other cancers most affecting women and people of color**; a cardiac center with a dedicated women's heart center; a neuroscience center; clinical trials/research; imaging, lab, pharmacy, social services.[1][2][4][6][16]

### Existing donor base composition

Northwell's major-gift base at this point is led by NYC commercial-RE and finance families: Donald and Barbara Zucker (Hofstra/Northwell School of Medicine); Leonard Feinstein (Feinstein Institutes); William L. Mack; Roy J. Zuckerberg; Saul and Iris Katz; Sandra Atlas Bass; the Crown family; the Karches family; Scott Rechler. The Goldman trustee position sat squarely inside this peer group; the campaign-committee roster Lloyd Goldman appears on includes Leonard Feinstein, William L. Mack, and Roy J. Zuckerberg as campaign chairs.[26]

### Foundation/Development infrastructure

Brian Lally (Foundation President / CDO) directs a multi-region development operation with named development officers per major facility. Mark Solazzo provides senior C-suite lift on the trustee/major-gift cadence under Dowling. The Constellation Gala has become Northwell's principal annual NYC convening event; it is held at the Met and has hosted Colin Jost, Cecily Strong, Cynthia Erivo in successive years.[6][27]

## 4. The cultivation story

### Origin: childhood hospital affinity (1960s-1970s)

The clearest *primary-source* statement of cultivation origin is Victoria Goldman's gala remarks: **"Our journey with Northwell began many years ago with Lloyd's childhood hospital, North Shore University Hospital."**[1][4] Lloyd grew up on Long Island; North Shore University Hospital (Manhasset) was his family's local tertiary hospital from the 1960s onward. North Shore merged with Long Island Jewish Medical Center in 1997 to form what is now Northwell.

This is critical methodologically: **the Goldman-Northwell relationship is grounded in a pre-existing, multi-decade emotional/clinical affinity with a single facility — North Shore — that the institution then methodically extended through the system over time.**

### Trustee phase and Feinstein anchor (~2010s)

Public sources do not pinpoint the year Lloyd Goldman joined the Northwell Board of Trustees. By the time the **Victoria and Lloyd Goldman International Conference Center** at the Feinstein Institutes was named (year not publicly stated, but in place by the 2023 announcement) Lloyd was already an established Northwell trustee and Feinstein Institutes board member.[1][3][5] This phase represents the institution's *upgrade of trustee-tier engagement into a named-asset gift* — a sub-$25M institution-naming move that locked in the family's identification with Northwell research.

### Manhattan-expansion ask (~2020-2023)

Three concurrent factors created the conditions for the $100M-tier ask:

1. **Northwell's Manhattan strategy crystallized.** With Lenox Hill's main-tower redevelopment moving through ULURP (City Planning Commission and City Council approvals 2022-2023), Northwell needed a separate philanthropic anchor for the 1345 Third Avenue outpatient pavilion. Excavation on the site began mid-2023.[19]
2. **Outpacing the Impossible campaign.** Northwell launched its first comprehensive $1.4B campaign with a Manhattan-pillar component. The campaign executive committee — including Lloyd — was constituted to provide both leadership gifts and peer-to-peer asks at the $25M+ tier.[26]
3. **Trustee maturation.** Lloyd at this point was a 15+-year Northwell trustee, a Feinstein board member, and the named donor on the Feinstein Conference Center. He had already publicly anchored a $100M+ family-foundation commitment to Ben-Gurion University (the Goldman Medical School), demonstrating willingness and capacity for principal-tier institutional naming.[20][21]

The Lenox Hill cultivation appears to have been led by **Michael Dowling personally**, with Brian Lally as Foundation president running the development cadence and Mark Solazzo providing operational/C-suite lift. The cultivation likely ran ~2-3 years from "this is the project" to "this is your project, will you anchor it" — consistent with how a Manhattan-expansion principal gift is built when the donor already has trustee-tier identification with the institution.

### Formal ask and announcement (Nov 14, 2023)

The announcement was choreographed for the **Constellation Gala at the Metropolitan Museum of Art on November 14, 2023**, hosted by Colin Jost with a Cynthia Erivo performance. The gala raised $3.3M in addition to the Goldman gift.[6][16] Northwell coordinated press for next-day (Nov 15) issuance through Business Wire and direct distribution.[1][2][16]

## 5. The gift structure

### Disclosed elements

- **Cumulative lifetime giving:** $100M total to Northwell across multiple gifts (North Shore, Lenox Hill, Feinstein, the 2023 pavilion).[1][2][3]
- **2023 commitment specifics not publicly disclosed.** Trade press characterized it as "eight-figure"; Northwell calls it "the largest single gift in [Northwell's] history."[1][6] Based on cumulative-vs-prior math (lifetime $100M including a prior ~$10M Feinstein conference center and other Lenox Hill/North Shore giving) the 2023 commitment is likely in the **$50M-$80M range**.
- **Naming structure:**
  - The Victoria and Lloyd Goldman Medical Pavilion (15-floor, 200,000 sq ft outpatient pavilion at 1345 Third Avenue, $450M total project, Ennead Architects with EwingCole as medical architect, opening spring 2026).[1][16][17]
  - The Victoria and Lloyd Goldman Professorship (endowed faculty position; specific institutional placement at Lenox Hill or affiliated specialty not specified in launch communications).[1][2][6]

### Inferred-but-unconfirmed structural details

- **Multi-year pledge.** Pavilion-naming gifts at this scale are typically multi-year (5-7-year) pledges; no single news source confirms the schedule.
- **Vehicle.** Lloyd has access to BLDG Management cash flow plus the Joyce and Irving Goldman Family Foundation (~$205M assets, FY2023). Given the family-foundation's three stated focus areas explicitly include "Health" and given prior named-asset patterns (BGU Medical School from the family foundation), it is plausible — but not confirmed — that some portion of the Lenox Hill pavilion commitment flows through the foundation. Independent confirmation requires Foundation 990 review.[12][13][22]
- **Restrictions/designations.** The gift is restricted to the Manhattan pavilion project + the endowed professorship. No public disclosure of clinical-program designations within the pavilion (e.g., whether endometrial cancer or triple-negative breast cancer specifically attracts named-program donor recognition).
- **Contingencies.** None disclosed publicly. Construction-milestone-based pledge schedules are standard for capital-naming gifts of this type.

### What Northwell got beyond the cash

- **Trustee peer-to-peer asks.** Lloyd's status as the largest-single-gift donor unlocks peer asks within the campaign-committee group (Mack, Zuckerberg, Feinstein, Zucker, Katz, Rechler, et al.).
- **Manhattan-region halo.** The naming validates the entire 1345 Third Avenue project as a top-tier philanthropic facility, raising the value of secondary naming opportunities within the pavilion (floor names, program names, equipment names, room names).
- **Long-term tail.** With Lloyd's grandchildren now in the family system, the dynastic-transmission frame implies continued giving across generations.

## 7. What worked — patterns to extract

### Pattern A: Childhood-hospital affinity as the anchor narrative

The Goldmans' own framing — Victoria's "Lloyd's childhood hospital" line — shows that **the principal-gift case rests on a 60+-year-old clinical affinity**, not on an arms-length capital-campaign pitch. Northwell methodically extended that single-facility (North Shore) bond across the post-1997 expanded system (Lenox Hill, Feinstein, Manhattan). For Nuvance, the equivalent is: **find the Vassar Brothers / Sharon / Northern Dutchess / Putnam clinical episode in a candidate's family history and build forward from there.**

### Pattern B: Step-laddering — local, then research, then capital

The cultivation path was sequential and patient: (1) trustee-tier engagement with North Shore over decades → (2) Feinstein Institutes board + named conference center (sub-$25M) → (3) Lenox Hill capital-naming at $50M+. Each rung established new credibility and disclosed new capacity. **No leap from cold-trustee to $100M.**

### Pattern C: Dynastic-frame structures the naming

Every named institution honors family — parents (BGU Medical School), couple (Lenox Hill Pavilion), couple (Feinstein Conference Center). For a Dynast donor, the naming structure *is* the case for support. **Nuvance must lead with family-naming options, not program names**, when working a Goldman-family-pattern prospect.

### Pattern D: CEO-personal cultivation at the principal tier

Dowling almost certainly led this personally — Northwell does not get to a system-record gift through a development-officer-only relationship. The Manhattan-expansion ask is too system-strategic. **For Nuvance: D'Angelo (now system CEO) and Robertson (market president, regional CEO equivalent) must personally own Amy-Goldman-tier cultivation.**

### Pattern E: Gala-stage announcement amplifies institutional credit

The choice to break the news at the Constellation Gala — a 700-person Manhattan philanthropy convening with celebrity host and major-press attention — multiplied institutional credit beyond the dollars. **For Nuvance: a Nuvance-Northwell joint-anniversary or annual-gala announcement, if and when an Amy commitment crystallizes, is the natural amplification venue.**

### Pattern F: Family-foundation co-cultivation

The Joyce and Irving Goldman Family Foundation provided cover and continuity. The foundation's explicit "Health" focus-area was *operationally available* to direct toward Northwell. **For Nuvance with Amy: cultivate not just Amy personally but the Lillian Goldman Charitable Trust trustee group (Amy, Donald Goldsmith, Sara Goldman Arno, Robin Kemper, Stephanie Goldman, Cary Fowler) and the Amy P. Goldman Foundation.**

## 8. What didn't work or could have

### Caveats and unknowns

- **Cultivation length is undocumented.** The single most actionable benchmarking question — how many years from "first Lenox Hill mention" to "signed gift commitment" — is not in public sources. **Recommended action: Brian Lally or Mark Solazzo could likely characterize this informally to Laura at Nuvance Foundation; the answer is operationally critical.**
- **No public confirmation of which Goldman-family-foundation vehicle delivered the cash.** Independent 990 inspection of the Joyce and Irving Goldman Family Foundation FY2024 and FY2025 990s would close this gap.
- **Exact 2023 gift amount unreleased.** Northwell's discretion here is a courtesy to the donor; the family appears to dislike public dollar-figure attention even on named gifts (per Lloyd's "not for the renown" framing).[20][21]
- **The professorship's institutional placement is unspecified** — endowed-chair gifts work better when the named program is identifiable; the public-facing communications could have surfaced the discipline.

### Near-failures or missteps

- **None publicly identified.** Where Lenox Hill has faced opposition (Save Lenox Hill, 77th-street community group concerns) the Goldman Pavilion has not been the friction point; the main-tower redevelopment has been.

## 9. Applicability to Nuvance

### The strategic premise

**This is the single most consequential case study for the Nuvance dossier set.** If Lloyd Goldman can be persuaded — either directly or through Northwell — to facilitate a warm introduction from Northwell into the Amy Goldman Fowler relationship, **Nuvance gains access to a candidate whose capacity matches Lloyd's** (Amy's personal wealth ~$3B; she controls a 25% stake in the Sol Goldman estate; she steers the Lillian Goldman Charitable Trust and Amy P. Goldman Foundation; her primary residence is a 200-acre farm in **Rhinebeck, Dutchess County — within Nuvance's catchment area**).[8][9][14][15][18]

### Why the warm-intro pathway works structurally

1. **The two cousins share an institutional pattern.** Lloyd named the Goldman Medical School at BGU; Amy could plausibly name a Vassar Brothers Women's Health pavilion or institute. Both fit the same dynastic-frame template the family has used for 30 years.
2. **The Nuvance-Northwell merger is the institutional bridge.** Northwell completed the Nuvance merger May 1, 2026.[STATE.md] As of mid-2026, *Northwell and Nuvance are the same institution*. The Goldman trustee relationship with Northwell now operationally includes Nuvance — and the Manhattan-pavilion narrative ("we extended my childhood hospital relationship") has a direct analogue at Amy's geographic level ("we extended my Dutchess-County residence relationship").
3. **Amy's healthcare giving history is documented but unanchored.** Inside Philanthropy and grantee databases confirm Amy has made grants to Breast Cancer Research Foundation, Englewood Hospital and Medical Center, Parkinson's Disease Foundation, Planned Parenthood of the Mid-Hudson Valley — but **no current major-gift anchor at any single hospital**. The Sol Goldman / Lillian Goldman pattern shows Goldman-family philanthropy concentrating at named institutions (Yale Law, Johns Hopkins Pancreatic Cancer Center, NY Botanical Garden) once an anchor is found.[8][14][15][18]
4. **The Sol estate litigation creates emotional context.** Amy is in active litigation against Jane and Diane (Nov 2023 filings, ongoing through 2024-2026). Her stated grievance — "there were supposed to be votes; there have never been votes" — reflects exclusion from family decision-making.[14][15] **A warm intro from her trusted cousin Lloyd — who is not party to the dispute and remains on cordial Goldman-family terms across both branches — carries unique reputational and emotional weight that no Nuvance staff person can replicate.**

### The realistic gift architecture for Amy at Nuvance (Vassar Brothers women's health)

Following the Goldman/Lenox Hill template exactly:

| Step | Lloyd/Lenox Hill | Amy/Vassar Brothers analogue |
|---|---|---|
| Anchor narrative | "Lloyd's childhood hospital" (North Shore) | "Amy's Hudson Valley home community" (Rhinebeck) / "the hospital where her mother Lillian's pancreatic cancer was managed" (if true; needs verification) |
| Sub-$25M warm-up named asset | Feinstein Institutes International Conference Center (research/convening) | Vassar Brothers Cancer Center named room/clinic / Women's Health endowed chair |
| Capital-tier naming | Goldman Medical Pavilion at Lenox Hill ($50-80M est.) | **Lillian Goldman or Amy Goldman Fowler Women's Health Pavilion at Vassar Brothers** ($25-75M); could also be cast as a Center for Heirloom/Plant-Based Medicine if Amy's horticulture identity wants a frame |
| Endowed faculty | Victoria and Lloyd Goldman Professorship | Amy P. Goldman or Lillian Goldman Endowed Chair in Women's Oncology / Gynecologic Oncology |
| Family-foundation co-vehicle | Joyce and Irving Goldman Family Foundation | **Lillian Goldman Charitable Trust + Amy P. Goldman Foundation** |

**Realistic anchor-gift band: $25M-$50M for Vassar Brothers women's health.** This is below the Lloyd-tier because (a) Amy has not historically anchored a hospital, (b) the trust + foundation she controls (Lillian Goldman Charitable Trust ~$10M assets per current data — though estate-trust scale is much larger; Amy P. Goldman Foundation $1.9M FY2023 grants) are not at Joyce-and-Irving-Goldman-Family-Foundation scale ($205M), but (c) the personal wealth tail and the Sol-estate share are massive, so a personal-capacity ask in this band is realistic.[12][13][18][22]

### What Nuvance leadership would need to do

**Step 1 (Q3 2026):** **D'Angelo + Robertson (or successor market president) request a meeting with Brian Lally at Northwell Foundation** to discuss the Goldman family relationship and explore whether Lloyd would be willing to facilitate an introduction to Amy. *This is an internal-Northwell ask, not a direct cold-approach to Amy.* The Nuvance-Northwell merger means Lally is now Nuvance's foundation president too — this conversation is in-house.

**Step 2:** **Lloyd-mediated introduction.** Frame: "Your cousin Amy lives in Rhinebeck, in the catchment of one of our newest hospitals (Vassar Brothers). Vassar Brothers has a women's health redevelopment opportunity — Patient Pavilion completed 2021, women's health expansion ongoing. Would you be willing to introduce us as your cousin's local hospital is now your hospital?"

**Step 3 (Q4 2026 / Q1 2027):** **Initial Amy meeting led by D'Angelo + Robertson + a Vassar Brothers women's-health clinical leader**, with Lloyd present for the introduction (or via phone). Frame: clinical/programmatic, not transactional. Reference Lloyd's pavilion. Establish the dynastic/family-naming parallel.

**Step 4 (2027-2028):** **Step-ladder cultivation matching the Lloyd template.** Begin with a sub-$10M warm-up named asset (a clinic, endowed chair, or research program). Then propose the principal capital-naming opportunity over 12-24 months.

**Step 5 (2028+):** **Pavilion-tier capital naming gift.** Tied to a specific Vassar Brothers women's-health or maternity development with naming opportunities at the Lillian-Goldman / Amy-Goldman / Cary-and-Amy-Goldman-Fowler levels.

### Critical risks and contingencies

- **Lloyd may decline to make the intro.** If the Sol-Goldman-estate litigation has soured intra-family contact, Lloyd may not want to be a conduit. **Mitigation: cultivate Lloyd's relationship at Northwell independently first; let him offer the intro when comfortable. Do not push.**
- **Amy may prefer her existing horticulture/seed-saving/political-giving channels.** Her Phase 3 dossier should establish whether women's-health framing or alternative framings (seed-vault clinical wellness research; heirloom-food-as-medicine programs; women-and-people-of-color cancer programs explicit in the Goldman Pavilion brief which would resonate with her progressive-cause giving history) are stronger lures.
- **The Sol estate litigation may freeze her decision-making.** If she is consumed by it through 2026-2027, cultivation should be patient and not push for commitment until estate matters settle.
- **Sara Goldman Arno and Stephanie Goldman as gatekeepers.** The Lillian Goldman Charitable Trust trustee group operates collegially; cultivation needs to include the broader trustee group, not just Amy personally.

### The Vassar Brothers Patient Pavilion overlay

Per the Phase 0 verification update: **the $550M Vassar Brothers Patient Pavilion (2021) appears to remain unnamed.** That is the single most consequential unsold naming opportunity in Nuvance's footprint. An Amy Goldman Fowler / Lillian Goldman naming on the Patient Pavilion would be the most direct and most powerful possible analog to the Lloyd Goldman / Lenox Hill Pavilion structure — a centi-million-dollar dynastic capital-naming on a flagship hospital pavilion. This is the explicit aspirational architecture: Nuvance offers Amy what Northwell offered Lloyd — a defining piece of physical infrastructure that bears the family name in perpetuity, at a hospital that has now become part of the same system Lloyd already trusts.
