## Tier framework — what each price band buys at a regional health system

Before the per-tier tables: a brief framework on how naming menus work at regional health systems (this is industry-standard knowledge from AHP benchmarks and peer-system published menus).

| Tier | What it typically names at a regional system | Industry observation |
|---|---|---|
| **$1M–$5M** | Patient-care floors, single endowed faculty positions ("Distinguished Physician"), waiting rooms, small named programs | Outside the scope of this analysis — too small to anchor a major-gift ask |
| **$10M** | Distinguished/endowed chairs (top end), named clinical programs, mid-size named spaces (ICU, surgical suite), named centers within an institute | Floor for "transformational" framing at most regional systems |
| **$25M** | Named departments (cardiology, oncology, etc.), mid-size named facilities (cancer center wings, women's pavilions), endowed chair clusters | Typical "lead gift to a campaign" tier at regional systems |
| **$50M** | Named institutes, named major clinical programs, named buildings of moderate scale, building wings at flagship facilities | Often the top-disclosed gift at regional systems; tier where Greenwich Hospital, Stamford Health, and Hartford operate as ceiling |
| **$100M** | Named buildings (hospital towers, full clinical buildings), named foundational institutes, named research enterprises | Rare at regional systems; typically requires a flagship academic medical center or a flagship community hospital in a wealthy market |
| **$250M+** | Whole-hospital naming, whole-system naming, named medical schools, named research enterprises spanning multiple buildings | Reserved for academic medical centers (Mount Sinai/Icahn, NYU/Langone, Penn) and the largest health systems (Northwell with $400M Sandra and Edward Meyer Cancer Center, etc.). Rare at the regional-system tier |

**Implication.** Nuvance can credibly anchor the **$10M–$50M** tiers with conventional naming. The **$100M** tier is achievable only against a named capital project (new tower, replacement hospital, or signature institute) — Nuvance must *have* such a project for that ask to be credible. The **$250M+** tier is, at face value, beyond what a regional system of Nuvance's current scale can name without bundling (e.g., whole-foundation naming with multi-decade endowment commitment).

## Strategic gaps

This is the most consequential section of this document. Surface honestly.

### Gap #1 — No publicly disclosed $100M+ capital project

Per the research constraints, this subagent could not confirm or rule out a planned $100M+ capital project at Nuvance. Based on the spec's institutional inventory and what is documented about Nuvance's hospital footprint, **the most likely strategic gap is that Nuvance does not currently have a tier-$100M+ capital project to name.**

**Why this matters for a $500M campaign.** Major capital campaigns at regional systems are typically anchored by *one* signature capital project that absorbs the lead gift ($50M–$150M) and provides a unifying narrative. Without such a project, a $500M aspiration becomes a sum of smaller projects across departments — institutionally credible but emotionally unanchored.

**Comparable peer signature projects:**
- Greenwich Hospital — the Helen and Charles Schwab Pavilion ($25M+ named) and the ongoing campus modernization
- Stamford Health — the new Stamford Hospital ($575M total project cost, opened 2016; multiple major-gift naming throughout)
- Hartford HealthCare — the Bone & Joint Institute and Hartford Hospital tower expansions
- Yale-New Haven — Smilow Cancer Hospital ($467M+ project, lead gift from Joel Smilow)
- Northwell — Sandra Atlas Bass Heart Hospital and multiple major capital projects

**What this means for the strategy package.** Either (a) Nuvance has a signature capital project that this subagent could not find (verification urgent), or (b) Nuvance needs to *define* one as a precondition to a $500M campaign. Option (b) is the right output for the case-for-support audit if (a) cannot be confirmed.

### Gap #2 — No nationally-recognized signature clinical program

Stamford Health markets neurosciences. Hartford markets cardiac and orthopedic. Greenwich markets women's-and-children's. Yale-New Haven markets Smilow Cancer. Each peer regional system has a *signature* clinical program that anchors its philanthropic identity.

Nuvance's strategic priorities per spec (cardiac, oncology, women's health, neurosciences) are broad — not a sharp signature. A $500M campaign typically requires the donor to feel they are giving to a *category-leading* asset; if Nuvance does not yet have one, the naming opportunities at the $50M+ tier are structurally weaker than peer institutions'.

**Implication for the case-for-support audit.** The audit must ask whether Nuvance leadership is willing to pick a signature program and invest behind it institutionally. Without that, $50M+ asks are competing against peer institutions that *do* have signature programs.

### Gap #3 — No research footprint to name

At academic medical centers, the research enterprise is itself a nameable asset (Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai, NYU Grossman School of Medicine, Perelman School of Medicine at Penn). Regional systems without research enterprises forfeit that entire naming category.

Nuvance has an early-stage research enterprise (limited published clinical-trial activity, no NIH R01 portfolio of consequence known to this subagent). A donor interested in being the founder of a research enterprise — the Phil Knight at OHSU model, in miniature — has no current institutional partner.

**Implication.** A specialized $100M+ ask configuration for a research-motivated donor (Investor face per Prince's typology) cannot currently be offered. This is a significant gap in the menu.

### Gap #4 — No medical school affiliation enables education-tier naming

Many of the largest hospital gifts in the past 15 years have named medical schools (Icahn, Grossman, Perelman, Geisel at Dartmouth, Zucker at Hofstra). Nuvance has affiliations with osteopathic and allopathic schools for clinical rotations [I — verification needed] but does not operate or co-own a medical school. The education-tier $250M+ naming category is closed.

### Gap #5 — Possible saturation at the $10M tier

If predecessor institutions (Vassar Brothers Health System, WCHN, Health Quest) ran active major-gift programs pre-merger — which is plausible given regional wealth — many of the lower-tier naming slots (distinguished chairs, named programs at flagship facilities) may already be taken. Task 0.4 will clarify; in the interim, the $10M-tier opportunity count quoted above (30–50 slots) may be optimistic.

### Gap #6 — Unclear naming-hierarchy decision-making post-merger

A 2019 merger creates governance complexity in naming decisions. If a donor wants to name a system-wide oncology institute, the decision touches CT-side trustees (WCHN legacy) and NY-side trustees (Health Quest legacy). Whether Nuvance has a clean naming-rights policy that scales to $50M+ commitments is unknown [U]. Without one, asks above $50M risk procedural delay that costs the foundation gift-window timing.

## Implications for per-candidate "ask" recommendations

This subagent's findings constrain the per-candidate "ask" recommendations that come later in Phase 5 (asks and stewardship) as follows:

1. **At the $10M–$25M tier**, naming opportunities are sufficient in volume. Per-candidate dossiers can confidently name a specific suggested asset (a chair, a program, an ICU, a service line at a specific facility).

2. **At the $50M tier**, only 3–4 naming opportunities should be actively marketed. Foundation strategy will need to decide which (most likely: Cancer Institute, Heart & Vascular Institute, Neuroscience Institute, Women's Health Institute — pick three). Per-candidate asks at this tier should be tied to specific service-line affinity.

3. **At the $100M tier**, asks are not credible without confirming a capital project. **For now, dossiers should hold $100M+ asks contingent on the case-for-support audit's findings about Nuvance's capital pipeline.** If no $100M+ project exists, $100M asks should be reframed as either (a) two $50M asks of different naming, (b) one $50M ask + multi-year endowment commitment for total $100M, or (c) a "founding family of [new institutional asset]" framing.

4. **At the $250M+ tier**, the empirical reality is that Nuvance currently cannot credibly offer a $250M+ named asset. Dossiers at this scale should be structured as **multi-decade family-philanthropy commitments** with the donor as founding family of a *new institutional capability* (research enterprise, children's hospital affiliate, etc.) — not as conventional naming.

5. **Strategic recommendation.** The case-for-support audit (Task 0.6) should explicitly recommend that Nuvance leadership define one $100M-tier signature capital project and one $50M-tier signature institute *before* the foundation begins formal $500M campaign solicitation. Without these two anchors, the campaign is institutionally premature.
