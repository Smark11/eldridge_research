# Phase 1 Screen 05: Private foundation principals — service-area address

**Compiled:** 2026-05-10
**Compiled by:** Subagent 5 (Phase 1.B), Nuvance major-gift prospect identification
**Method:** WebSearch on ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer + Cause IQ + Instrumentl + Inside Philanthropy + direct foundation sites; 990-PF data cross-referenced with news coverage of healthcare giving and family-office structures.
**Scope reminder:** This screen surfaces principals of *private foundations* with service-area addresses (Dutchess, Putnam, Western Fairfield, Litchfield counties). Subagents 1-4 cover Forbes/Bloomberg, hedge fund executives, and local press separately. Where overlap is unavoidable (e.g., a hedge-fund manager whose primary identifier is *also* his foundation address), I've flagged it for cross-reference rather than excluding.

---

## Inclusion threshold applied

Per screening_methodology.md: foundation assets **≥ $10M** AND service-area address. I have applied the $10M threshold strictly to surface the foundations themselves; for *principals*, I also note any major documented healthcare gifts (the Bauer case below sits below the foundation-asset threshold but its principal donors gave $35M+ to Norwalk Hospital direct, making them a Phase 1 candidate regardless).

## Notes and data gaps

1. **Dyson Foundation Schedule I details for FY 2023/2024:** ProPublica has the full 990-PF available but the Schedule I (grants-paid line items) was not extractable via search summary. To get precise grant-by-grant Vassar Brothers giving for the last 5 years, a manual PDF review of the FY2020-FY2024 Schedule I filings is needed (one ProPublica filing URL is documented above). Phase 2 should do this.

2. **John and Kathe Dyson Foundation (Rob's brother John):** Filed 990-PF for 2021/2022/2023 but asset totals not surfaced by web search. Manual ProPublica filing-by-filing review needed — this is a meaningful gap because John S. Dyson is a wealthy second Dyson sibling and could double the family ask capacity.

3. **The Whittingham Family Foundation reported $0 assets:** This is suspicious — likely the foundation operates as a pure flow-through pass from family wealth rather than holding an endowment. This is common for highly active living donors. Funding capacity should be assessed via family wealth not via foundation balance sheet.

4. **Andrew J Whittingham Family Foundation (separate, 2023):** Newer foundation in build-out phase. This may indicate succession planning — the new gen-2 vehicle is being capitalized — and is a high-confidence succession-stage signal.

5. **Bauer Foundation succession risk:** George Bauer is ~95 years old (b. 1931). The next $20M gift was 2022. Carol Bauer remains Emeritus Trustee. **High urgency for engagement** — succession transitions are imminent. Next-generation Bauer children include at least one Wilton resident. Cultivation strategy should include Bauer descendants and frame future giving as continuing the family legacy.

6. **Greenwich-area billionaire foundations (Asness, Tudor, Mandel, Halvorsen, Och, Hutchins):** All have service-area Greenwich addresses but most do NOT have documented Nuvance-facility giving. Their healthcare patterns are flagship-NYP/MSK or Greenwich-Hospital/Yale-NHH (competitor-aligned). They are billionaire-tier wealth, but per Phase 0 calibration billionaires are long-shots for Nuvance unless very strong specific affinity exists. Most should be flagged as MEDIUM priority and assessed in Phase 2 only if affinity-first centi-millionaire pool is thin.

7. **Litchfield County:** Surprisingly thin private-foundation density at the $10M+ asset threshold. Largest finds: Low Road Foundation ($217M, Jasper Johns, Sharon), Tauck Family Foundation ($26M, Wilton — adjacent not Litchfield). Most other Litchfield/Hotchkiss-orbit foundations are sub-$10M family-name foundations (Findlay $700K, Shelley, Borman, etc.). Litchfield's wealth structure runs more toward weekender private wealth + DAFs + direct giving than via locally-domiciled private foundations.

8. **Westchester County (NY) service-area towns (Bedford, Pound Ridge, Scarsdale, Mt. Kisco, Chappaqua, Bronxville, Rye, Larchmont):** Surveyed — most foundations identified are sub-$5M family vehicles (Pollack, Schwartz, Kurtz, etc.). No major private foundations >$50M identified in this survey for those zips. Wealth in these towns runs more toward NYC-domiciled foundations or DAFs.

9. **Putnam County, NY:** No major principal-led private foundations >$10M identified with primary Putnam County address. Putnam wealth tends to be commuter-pattern with NYC tax address.

10. **DAF opacity:** Multiple billionaires (Mandel via Zoom, others) channel >99% of giving through Fidelity Charitable or similar DAFs — making their actual cause-allocation effectively invisible. This is a methodological limit noted in screening_methodology.md and applies here.

11. **990-PF lag:** Data lag is typically 18-24 months for full filings; FY2024 data is the most recent generally available for foundations on calendar-year cycles.
