Long-List Screening Methodology.

Compiled: 2026-05-10 Authoritative inputs:

  • Project spec: docs/superpowers/specs/2026-05-10-nuvance-fundraising-strategy-design.md
  • Phase 0 verification update: sources/phase_0_verification_update.md
  • Case-for-support audit: research/phase_0_institutional_context/case_for_support_audit.md

Scope refinement (per Checkpoint A)

The original spec called for 10 final candidates (4-5 billionaires + 5-6 centi-millionaires). At Checkpoint A, the project owner refined the scope:

  • Final candidate set: 5-7 highest-affinity candidates (was 10)
  • Calibration: affinity-first centi-millionaires dominate (Phase 0 audit verdict: billionaires will rationally route to Northwell flagships post-merger; centi-millionaires with Hudson Valley / Western CT ties are the structurally correct segment)
  • Realistic gift target: $75-200M cumulative from 2-3 transformational gifts over 3-5 years (was: "contributing meaningfully to a $500M aspiration" — the audit verdict made clear the institutional case for $500M is not currently sustained)

The long-list target adjusts accordingly: 25-40 scored candidates (was 40-60), giving Phase 2 short-list selection (down to 5-7) sufficient optionality without inflating Phase 1 effort.

Inclusion criteria

A candidate enters the long list if they meet ALL of:

  1. Estimated net worth ≥ $300M (verified against ≥ 2 independent sources where possible; ≥ 1 source mandatory)
  2. At least one of the following Nuvance-affinity signals:
    • Primary residence in Nuvance core service area (Dutchess, Putnam, Western Fairfield, Litchfield counties)
    • Secondary residence in the core service area
    • Primary business operations in the service area
    • Board service at any Nuvance facility or predecessor institution (current or past)
    • Documented patient history at any Nuvance facility (rare — typically only via news/obit references)
    • Multigenerational legacy family at any Nuvance predecessor institution (e.g., Dyson, Whittingham, or families with named buildings/programs)
  3. Not on the documented exclusion list (see disqualifying factors below)

A candidate is also added (without requiring service-area connection) if they meet:

  • Estimated net worth ≥ $1B AND any past hospital/medical gift ≥ $5M anywhere (billionaires are mobile and may consider non-flagship hospitals if affinity is built; but realistic calibration says they're long-shots vs. centi-millionaires for Nuvance specifically)

Affinity-signal scoring (1-5 per signal, six signals, total 6-30)

S1 — Documented Nuvance connection

  • 5: Board service at a Nuvance entity or predecessor (current or past); major documented patient experience; named gift to a Nuvance facility
  • 4: Multigenerational legacy family at a Nuvance predecessor institution (e.g., Dyson at Vassar Brothers, Whittingham at Norwalk) — but with the family already engaged
  • 3: Spouse, sibling, or parent has had any of the above
  • 2: Employee, vendor, or business-relationship connection to a Nuvance entity
  • 1: No direct connection but documented use of Nuvance services as a community member

S2 — Residential ties

  • 5: Primary residence in core service area (Dutchess, Putnam, western Fairfield, Litchfield counties)
  • 4: Primary residence in core service area for ≥ 5 years (deeper roots)
  • 3: Secondary residence in core service area, or primary residence in adjacent county
  • 2: Secondary residence in adjacent county only, or family ties to area
  • 1: Commuter relationship only (primary NYC residence, weekday-only or weekender pattern)

S3 — Healthcare giving history

  • 5: Named gift ≥ $10M to any hospital or medical institution (proves transformational-scale willingness)
  • 4: Cumulative documented healthcare giving ≥ $5M; or one gift $5-10M
  • 3: Cumulative healthcare giving $1-5M; or active multi-year healthcare-cause foundation giving
  • 2: Documented minor healthcare giving ($100K-$1M cumulative)
  • 1: Minimal or no documented healthcare giving

S4 — Cause affinity to Nuvance signature programs

Aligned with Phase 0 findings on what Nuvance can plausibly position as signature programs post-Northwell merger.

  • 5: Documented giving to one of Nuvance's strongest current clinical areas (cancer, cardiac, women's health, neurosciences); especially if to a regional/community hospital rather than flagship
  • 4: Giving to comparable hospital cause but at a flagship (NYP, MSK) — suggests cause affinity but competing loyalty
  • 3: Giving to related healthcare causes (research, education, public health)
  • 2: Giving to general nonprofits in the region (community foundation, local university) but not healthcare-specific
  • 1: Unrelated cause profile

S5 — Giving vehicle sophistication

  • 5: Active private foundation with ≥ $50M in assets, multi-year grant history
  • 4: Active private foundation with $10-50M in assets; or large active DAF
  • 3: Smaller private foundation ($1-10M) with active grant history; or small DAF
  • 2: Foundation exists but inactive; or giving solely through direct donations
  • 1: Minimal philanthropic infrastructure (no foundation, no DAF, only occasional direct giving)

S6 — Life-stage signals

  • 5: Age 65+ AND recent liquidity event (within 36 months) AND clear succession-planning behavior (foundation activity transitions, family-office hires)
  • 4: Any two of: age 65+, recent liquidity event, succession-planning behavior
  • 3: One of: age 65+, recent liquidity event, succession-planning behavior
  • 2: Mid-career (age 45-64) with established wealth, no clear succession-planning yet
  • 1: Young (under 45), early-career wealth, no near-term liquidity or succession signals

Scoring outcome thresholds

  • ADVANCE to Phase 2 short-list consideration: total score ≥ 18
  • HOLD (borderline): total score 15-17 — review during Phase 2 if ADVANCE pool is thin
  • DROP: total score < 15

Given the scope refinement, the expected ADVANCE pool is 25-40 candidates. If the screen produces:

  • < 25: methodology may be too strict; review HOLD candidates (15-17) for inclusion
  • > 40: methodology produced too many; tighten scoring or apply stricter affinity weighting

Disqualifying factors (override otherwise-qualifying candidates)

A candidate is excluded entirely if any of the following apply:

  1. Documented public hostility toward Nuvance, Northwell, or a predecessor institution
  2. Active competitor in late-stage solicitation AT A NORTHWELL-COMPETING INSTITUTION (Memorial Sloan Kettering, Cleveland Clinic, etc.) where Nuvance approaching would be counterproductive. Note: late-stage solicitation at a NON-COMPETING institution (e.g., Yale-NHH, Penn Medicine) is less disqualifying since donors can give to both; flag but don't exclude.
  3. Recent declined asks at ≥ 2 comparable institutions for unrelated reasons (signals not philanthropically active right now)
  4. Active litigation or public controversy that would create reputational risk for Nuvance/Northwell to associate with publicly
  5. Wealth is structurally illiquid with no near-term liquidity path AND no active foundation or DAF (no way to give from)
  6. Already gave a transformational gift to a flagship within 24 months (signal that giving capacity for next-tier-flagship-equivalent is exhausted in near term)

Wealth-tier calibration for the long list

Given the Phase 0 audit verdict and the scope refinement to 5-7 final candidates:

  • Centi-millionaires ($300M-$999M): aim for ~70-80% of long list — these are the strategically correct segment for Nuvance post-merger
  • Billionaires ($1B+): aim for ~20-30% of long list — included for completeness but with lower probability of conversion; only those with very strong Nuvance-specific affinity make it

This shifts the calibration MORE toward centi-millionaires vs. the original spec (which was 50/50 billionaires/centi-millionaires).

Phase 0-informed priors

Specific findings from Phase 0 that inform Phase 1 screening:

  1. Whittingham family is already a top Phase 1 prospect. $4.5M cumulative giving since 1997 to Norwalk Hospital cancer care, multi-generational (Andrew and Michael Whittingham now active), classic Repayer motivation. Add to long list automatically; full affinity-scoring still applies.

  2. Dyson Foundation / Dyson family is an established multi-decade $1M-tier donor at Vassar Brothers. Robert R. Dyson is alive and Chairman. Add to long list automatically; but acknowledge transformational ask requires significant escalation from historical scale.

  3. Hudson Valley grandee corridor (potential Astor, Roosevelt, Delano, Aldrich descendants) flagged in Phase 0; verify which living individuals exist and have the wealth + affinity profile.

  4. Greenwich / Fairfield hedge fund executive cohort — Western Fairfield County has the highest density of ultra-wealthy individuals in the U.S.; screen aggressively for primary-residence signal.

  5. GE / Pepsi / Fortune 500 executive cohorts with HV/Western CT ties — historical executive cohort residence in service area; many are now in their 60s-80s and in succession-planning life stage.

  6. Litchfield County Hotchkiss-orbit families — Sharon Hospital constituency; potential affinity for community-leveraged philanthropy.

  7. Vassar College alumni network — institutional connection to Vassar Brothers via the historical Vassar family link; alumni philanthropy patterns may include the hospital.

Source rigor requirements (hard standard)

Per project owner feedback at Checkpoint A:

  • Every factual claim in screening output must have an inline source citation (URL or filing identifier + retrieval date)
  • Wealth estimates must be triangulated against ≥ 2 independent sources
  • Affinity-signal scores must reference the specific evidence that informed each score
  • Confidence labels ((known) / (inferred) / (unknown)) applied to all material claims
  • No "reportedly" or unsourced assertions

Methodology limitations (transparent acknowledgment)

  • DAF grants are not public. We can identify that a candidate has a DAF (sometimes), but not their grants out of it.
  • Wealth estimates for non-Forbes-listed individuals are inferred, typically accurate within a factor of 2x.
  • Anonymous gifts are common at the mega-gift level — some giving will be invisible.
  • 990-PF data lags 18-24 months — recent activity may not yet appear.
  • Family-office structures are often opaque — not all family-office philanthropy infrastructure is publicly discoverable.
  • The pre-merger Nuvance vs. post-merger Northwell-Nuvance distinction creates ambiguity in some affinity claims that will need to be resolved in dossier work.
